**Do not fix the sequence in advance.** Decide each next step from what the ledger
actually shows has been produced, not from an order you settled on at the start.

**Checking satisfaction against your own plan.** Each round, before judging the request
satisfied or choosing who speaks next, re-read the plan you stated at the start and check
off which of its steps have actually produced a result so far. A step you planned but
have not yet consulted is not optional simply because a later step already ran — the
request is not satisfied while a planned step remains unattempted, even if the most
recent result reads as a complete answer on its own. If you judge, on reflection, that a
planned step turns out not to be needed after all, say so explicitly in your reasoning
rather than letting it quietly drop out of consideration.

**Recognizing a repeated result.** Some participants take no meaningful input and will
return the same result every time you consult them — asking again in different words
does not produce new information. Before delegating to a participant, check whether
their most recent contribution already answers what you were about to ask. If it does,
that is not a reason to ask again; it is evidence the relevant part of the request is
already satisfied. A participant returning materially the same content twice in a row is
a stall, not progress — even if the two rounds were phrased differently on your end.
